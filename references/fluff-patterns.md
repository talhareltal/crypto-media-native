# Fluff patterns

Things reporters see a hundred times a day. Each one costs you credibility.

## Words that signal nothing

- First, leading, largest, premier, top-tier (unless proven)
- Revolutionary, game-changing, groundbreaking, next-generation
- Strategic (partnership, investment, collaboration)
- Ecosystem growth, mass adoption, bridging Web2 and Web3
- Empower, unlock, accelerate, leverage
- Seamless, robust, cutting-edge

Rule: if removing the word doesn't change the meaning, remove it.

## Structural problems

- **Future tense doing the work.** "Will enable", "plans to", "set to". Report what happened.
- **Unnamed counterparts.** "A top-five exchange", "leading institutions". Name them or cut it.
- **Numbers without a source.** "Over 1M users". Defined how? Checked where?
- **The quote is the content.** If the CEO's quote is the only substance, there's no story.
- **Buried lead.** The real news is in paragraph four. Move it to the headline.
- **Boilerplate longer than the news.**
- **Embargo on nothing.** If nothing is time-sensitive, an embargo is just friction.

## Crypto-specific fluff

- TVL as a growth metric when it moves with token price
- Wallet counts that include airdrop farmers
- "Community" numbers from Telegram and Discord with no engagement data
- Token burns framed as product news
- Unlocks framed as "ecosystem expansion"
- "Backed by" a fund that put in a small check years ago
- Listings on exchanges nobody trades on
- "Partnership" that means you're on their wallet list
- Borrowed credibility: big brand names you can pay at via card rails, listed like partners
- Boilerplate doing the work: investor lists and volume stats about the partner, not the news
- No link to a live product anywhere in the release

## Pitch-specific problems

- Subject line that doesn't say the news
- First two lines are about the company, not the news
- "Exclusive" sent to more than one reporter
- No reason why this reporter, why now
- Attachments instead of a link
- Follow-up after 24 hours on a non-urgent story

## How to fix a draft

Return:
1. The real headline in under 12 words
2. What to cut
3. What's missing
4. The rewritten first paragraph, if they ask
