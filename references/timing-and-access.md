# Timing and access

How the news goes out matters as much as what it is.

## Exclusive, embargo or blast

**Exclusive:** one reporter, one outlet, first. Use when the news is good but not huge, and one strong story beats ten weak mentions.
- Offer it to one at a time. Wait for a no before the next.
- Say clearly it's an exclusive and when the window ends.
- Never offer the same exclusive to two people. You will get caught. Crypto media is small.

**Embargo:** several reporters get it early, all publish at the same time. Use when the news is big enough that multiple outlets want it.
- Get agreement before sending details.
- Embargo time in UTC and the reporter's time zone.
- Only for genuinely time-sensitive news.

**Blast:** release goes out to everyone at once. Use for straightforward announcements and when you need it on record. Expect less coverage.

## Timing

- Avoid Fridays and weekends for anything you want covered. Exceptions: crypto never sleeps, but reporters do.
- Watch the market. A big market move, major hack or regulatory headline will bury your news. Be ready to delay.
- Mind time zones. US morning catches US and Europe. Asia morning catches Asia and late Europe.
- Avoid major conference weeks unless you're announcing at the conference.
- Give reporters lead time: a few days for an exclusive, more for a feature.

## What reporters need from you

- The news in one sentence
- Numbers they can print
- A spokesperson available that day, not next week
- Someone outside the company who'll confirm (investor, partner, customer)
- Assets: logo, headshot, one chart or screenshot
- Answers to the obvious hard question before they ask it

## Red flags in your own plan

- "We'll send it to everyone and see who picks it up"
- Announcing before it's live
- Spokesperson unavailable on launch day
- Partner hasn't approved the wording
- Legal hasn't seen it, and it touches tokens or regulation
